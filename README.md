# US-Navy-SH-60B-Helmet-Audio-Cord-Adapter
SH-60B Helmet Audio Cord Adapter: U-384/U to U-174/U

There are products available that allow people to use their military aviation helmets with their PCs such as the FlightSounds' Solo-M military headset adapter (https://flightsounds.com/products/solo-m-military).  These systems generally accept the U-174/U, which is a 4-conductor audio jack.  Unfortunately, SH-60B helmets used the U-384/U, a 5-conductor audio jack, to plug in to the helicopter's communication system.  They also had a 27.5 volt DC amplifier on the microphone line.  These helmets will work with the FlightSounds system, but you have to do two things.  

First, you must bypass the amplifier.  This is a simple matter of unplugging the microphone's two-pin connector from the amplifier, unplugging the amplifier's the two-pin connector from the base of the helmet, and then plugging in the microphone's two-pin connector to the base of the helmet.

Second, you have to create an adapter cable to change your helmet's 5-conductor audio jack (U-384/U) into a 4-conductor audio jack (U-174/U).  For this you will need a U-385/U receptacle (https://www.mouser.com/en/ProductDetail/Amphenol-Nexus-Technologies/U-385-U?qs=NoqMqJKvPFIWB0bzhNX%252BGA%3D%3D) that you plug your helmet into.  You then wire that to a U-174/U jack (https://www.pilotshq.com/products/david-clark-electrical-connector-09226p-02) that will plug into your PC's headset adapter.

The wiring diagram below shows how the conductors should be mapped in order for your helmet to work with the FlightSounds Solo-M adapter:

<img width="408" height="347" alt="adapter-diagram" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/0a933200-d6fa-4e08-bb77-a1b4705f486e" />

Using a 4-wire cable (https://www.amazon.com/Conductor-Electrical-Stranded-Extension-Automotive/dp/B0CFJXMDT3) that is long enough to reach from your helmet to the PC headset adapter, connect the U-385/U receptacle to the U-174/U jack according to the mapping above.  If you look closely at the base of the wiring pins on the receptacle and jack, you should see the number corresponding to the conductor.

Notice how conductor 2 on the U-385/U is connected to conductor 2 on the U-174/U jack.  Also note that conductor 1 on the U-174/U is the center post as shown in the above image. WORD OF ADVICE:  Make sure you put the plastic covers for the receptacle and jack on the 4-wire cable BEFORE you start soldering!
